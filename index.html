Discord HQ
Discord is our primary communication platform and the heartbeat of the Cioré contributor community. This guide will help you set up your profile properly, navigate the server with confidence, and understand how we communicate as a team. Whether you are brand new or just looking to get your settings right, start here.
Display Name Setup

Your display name in the Cioré HQ server should follow this format so the team knows who you are and how to support you:

First Name - Role
Example: Jordan - UX Designer Contributor

This helps every contributor and the founder instantly recognize who they are talking to and what kind of work you do. It also helps project managers reach the right people quickly.

How to Update Your Display Name

If you already have a Discord account, use the per-server profile feature so your changes only apply to Cioré HQ and do not affect your other servers.

Step 01
Open the Cioré HQ Server
Navigate to the Cioré HQ server in your Discord sidebar.
Step 02
Open Your Profile Settings
Click on your avatar or username. Look for the option to edit your server profile or per-server nickname.
Step 03
Select Cioré HQ
Make sure you are editing the profile for this server specifically, not your global Discord profile.
Step 04
Update Your Display Name
Set your display name to match the format: First Name - [Role] Contributor. Save your changes.

What Is Cioré HQ?

Cioré HQ is the name of our Discord server. Think of it as our virtual headquarters, the place where contributor activity lives, conversations happen, and the team stays connected. Just as the Contributor Hub is where you go to read and learn, Cioré HQ on Discord is where you go to communicate and collaborate.

Server, Categories, and Channels

If Discord is new to you, here is a quick breakdown of how it is organized:

Level 1
Server
The overall private space for the organization, like an online office building. Cioré HQ is our server. You need an invitation to join.
Level 2
Category
A grouped section of related channels, like a floor or department in a building. Categories help keep conversations organized by topic or team.
Level 3
Channel
An individual room for a specific purpose, announcements, project discussion, ideas, meetings, or general conversation. Each channel has a stated purpose.
  • Some channels are announcement-only, you can read but not post.
  • Some channels are project-specific, focused on active initiatives.
  • Some channels are role-gated, only visible or accessible based on your contributor role.
How to Navigate
  • Use the left sidebar in Discord to move between categories and channels.
  • You can collapse and expand categories by clicking the category name.
  • When looking for a specific channel, check the server map or channel guide if one is available.
  • To stay updated, always check announcement channels first, that is where important notices live.
Where to Start

When you first join, head to the Welcome or Announcements channel. That is where important updates, reminders, and notices are posted. Start there, read through what is available, and then explore the rest of the server at your own pace.

Important Channels to Know

Communication Expectations

We are a distributed, globally connected team. That means communication across time zones, backgrounds, and schedules. Please keep these expectations in mind as you participate in Cioré HQ:

  • Before messaging someone, take a moment to check their role, pronouns, DM preference, and time zone in their server profile.
  • Please be respectful of how people identify and communicate. Use correct names and pronouns.
  • Be considerate of different time zones and response windows. Not everyone is available at the same hours.
  • Meetings are scheduled in Central Time unless otherwise noted. Use a time zone translator to confirm your local time.
  • Use channels for their intended purpose whenever possible. If you are unsure where something belongs, ask in a general channel or check the server map.
  • When in doubt, default to respect. This community is built on care, intentionality, and mutual support.
If you are ever unsure about something, message the founder directly on Discord. We would rather you ask than feel lost. Welcome to Cioré HQ.

Code of Conduct

By accepting your contributor access and participating in any Cioré workstream, you are agreeing to uphold everything in this document. You do not need to sign a separate form. Your participation is your agreement.

ⓘ
This Code of Conduct applies to all contributors in all spaces connected to Cioré, including Discord, the Contributor Hub, calls, emails, and any public or private communication done in connection with your role here.
1. Why This Exists

Cioré is a community-driven startup built on trust, transparency, and a shared belief that technology should serve people. This code exists to protect that environment. It sets clear expectations so every contributor knows how to show up, what is expected, and what this community will and will not tolerate. We are building something meaningful together. This is how we make sure the process reflects the product.

2. Core Values
Respect
Treat Everyone Well
Treat every contributor with kindness, empathy, and fairness. This applies in Discord, in documents, in meetings, and in every written message.
Professionalism
Follow Through
Communicate clearly. Follow through on what you commit to. If something comes up, say so early rather than going quiet.
Integrity
Be Honest
Be honest about your work, your capacity, and any concerns you have. If you see something that does not feel right, bring it to the founder.
Inclusivity
Everyone Belongs
Cioré is built for and by people from all backgrounds. Differences in experience, identity, and perspective make this team stronger.
Alignment
Uphold the Mission
You are here because you believe in what we are building. Uphold that mission in how you work, what you say publicly, and how you represent this community.
3. How We Work Together
  • Be professional in all interactions, whether in Discord, email, documents, or calls.
  • Communicate early if you need more time or run into a challenge. We would rather know than wonder.
  • Use the designated Cioré channels for all project communication. Keep work conversations in the right spaces.
  • Do not use offensive, discriminatory, or harassing language or imagery anywhere connected to Cioré.
  • Be open to feedback. Assume good intent. Encourage honest, constructive conversations.
  • Recognize others. If someone does good work, say so. We are a team.
  • Do not take credit for contributions that are not yours.
4. Confidentiality

What happens inside Cioré stays inside Cioré.

  • Do not share internal documents, design files, product details, contributor information, or any internal discussions outside of the team without explicit written permission from the founder.
  • This includes anything in this hub, anything shared in Discord, and anything discussed in contributor calls or meetings.
  • Confidentiality does not end when your contributor role does. These obligations remain in effect after you leave.
  • If you are unsure whether something can be shared externally, ask the founder before sharing it.
5. Data and Tools
  • Use only approved Cioré tools and systems for all work-related communications, file storage, and collaboration.
  • Do not store Cioré materials in personal accounts, unauthorized apps, or anywhere outside of approved systems.
  • Comply with all applicable data privacy standards. If your role involves any contributor or user data, handle it with care.
6. Intellectual Property
  • Work you create specifically for Cioré as part of your contributor role belongs to Cioré unless otherwise agreed in writing.
  • Your own personal work, created outside of and unrelated to Cioré, remains yours.
  • You may include non-confidential examples of your Cioré contributions in a personal portfolio with prior approval from the founder.
  • Do not use Cioré name, trademarks, logos, or branding without explicit authorization.
7. Conflicts of Interest

We ask for transparency, not perfection.

  • Disclose any outside relationships, employment, or activities that could reasonably conflict with Cioré interests.
  • Do not use your contributor role to promote unrelated products, services, or organizations.
  • Do not engage in work that directly competes with Cioré during your time as a contributor and for one year after your role ends.
  • Do not solicit other Cioré contributors, employees, or partners away from the organization.
  • If you are ever unsure whether something is a conflict, bring it to the founder. There is no penalty for asking.
8. Harassment and Discrimination

Cioré has zero tolerance for harassment, discrimination, or bullying in any form.

This includes unwelcome comments, slurs, jokes, or behavior targeting anyone based on race, gender, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, national origin, or any other characteristic. It applies in public channels, direct messages, calls, and any space where Cioré contributors interact.

⚠
If you experience or witness anything that violates this, report it to the founder directly. All reports are treated confidentially and taken seriously. No one will face retaliation for reporting in good faith.
9. Accountability

We are a startup. Things are still being built, processes are still forming, and not everything will be perfect. We ask for your patience and we extend the same to you. What we do expect is that everyone holds themselves to the standards in this document.

  • Violations may result in removal from a project or from your contributor role entirely.
  • Serious violations involving data misuse, harassment, or theft of intellectual property may result in legal action.
  • All concerns are reviewed confidentially and handled fairly.
10. Your Agreement
By accepting your contributor access and participating as an active contributor to Cioré, you confirm that you have read, understood, and agree to uphold this Code of Conduct. Your participation is your agreement. If anything in this document is unclear, reach out to the founder before proceeding.

Phone System Overview
Cioré is exploring a privacy-first, modular smartphone ecosystem made of 4 integrated research directions.
Official Concept View
First Official Concept
Cioré Phone Mockup

This is the first official concept view of the Cioré phone. It is being shared internally to give contributors a visual reference for the current product direction, industrial design language, and overall feel of the device.

This concept represents an early but intentional design milestone. As prototype development continues, some details may evolve through engineering, sourcing, usability, and materials decisions.

What We Are Working Toward
Direction 01
The Phone
A transparent, recycled-glass, modular smartphone concept with screw-focused assembly, visible internal structure, and a visual interface emphasizing calm precision, clean surfaces, and intentional tactile controls.
Direction 02
Lumé OS
A privacy-first operating environment built on a hardened open foundation. Cioré is researching custom privacy rules, permissions, and user experience. The near-term MVP uses an AOSP base for testing, subject to ongoing technical validation.
Direction 03
Nyro
Nyro is Cioré's energy and thermal research system. Cioré is exploring ways to help users depend less on wall charging through small supplemental energy inputs from light and warmth, alongside safer charging, battery longevity, thermal comfort, and repairability research. USB-C remains the primary charging method.
Direction 04
Gaia
Cioré is studying Gaia as a transparency and user-control research direction that may help people better understand AI-related activity inside apps and consider available choices.
Consumer Problems We Solve
  • Pervasive data exploitation: phones and apps track location, behaviors, and contacts, monetizing users as products rather than customers.
  • Forced financial upgrades: performance throttling and unsupported OS versions push users into buying new $800–$1,200 phones every 2–3 years.
  • Massive e-waste: 62 million tonnes of e-waste were generated in 2022, projected to reach 82 million tonnes by 2030, with only ~22% formally recycled.
  • Unethical supply chains: many phone batteries depend on cobalt and nickel from conflict zones and child labor-heavy mines.
  • Hidden environmental costs: producing one smartphone can emit as much CO2 as driving ~100 miles, while devices often end up landfilled prematurely.
Business Problems We Solve

Organizations handling sensitive information face serious risks from mainstream consumer phones:

  • Employee Data Leaks: staff mixing client data with ad-tracking apps and unvetted cloud backups creates constant exposure risk.
  • Compliance Challenges: standard phones log and sync heavily, making GDPR, HIPAA, and financial compliance audits painful and expensive.
  • Targeted Executive Attacks: C-level leaders face SIM swaps, phishing, and spyware aimed at deal intel and strategic plans.
  • Client Trust at Risk: a single leaked call, message, or document can destroy years of professional reputation.
  • Remote Teams Exposed: staff on untrusted networks relying on tracker-loaded devices creates organizational blind spots.
  • Rising Insurance Premiums: cyber insurers raise rates or deny coverage when fleets rely on generic consumer devices without verifiable hardware-level security.
Who We Protect
Individuals
Everyday users tired of being tracked and profiled who want something fundamentally different. People who want to keep a phone for 5+ years, repair parts, and avoid wasteful cycles. Eco-conscious consumers actively choosing sustainable, ethically sourced products.
Professionals
Journalists, lawyers, therapists, and doctors who require built-in confidentiality without complex setups. Ethical tech leaders and executives in climate tech, cybersecurity, ESG, and nonprofits who need devices aligned with their public values. Investigative fieldworkers, reporters, and NGO staff who must minimize location tracking and metadata exposure.
Organizations
Corporate risk and compliance teams needing verifiably secure device fleets that pass audits and reduce liability. Remote IT and security teams maintaining secure fleets for distributed staff in high-risk environments. Institutional ESG buyers including universities, nonprofits, and impact-driven enterprises seeking ethically sourced hardware.
Industrial Design and Form Factor

Cioré is exploring a calm, precise, premium-feeling device and interface experience with clean surfaces, intentional controls, tactile feedback, and clear visual hierarchy. Every edge, curve, and surface is considered in research models.

DimensionTarget Benchmark
Length155–165 mm (standard research reference class)
Width70–75 mm
Thickness7–9 mm at center, tapering toward edges for visual thinness
Corner Radius4–5 mm softly rounded corners
Long-Edge Curvature2–3 mm radius to evaluate hand fatigue reduction
Shape and Ergonomics: Continuous curved slab concepts with smooth side transitions. Front glass is slightly convex in conceptual models. soft curves prevent pressure points during testing. Asymmetrical bevel concepts act as a rest zone for thumb placement during single-handed ergonomics trials.
Materials: Recycled Glass and Nanocellulose

The structural body research focuses on recycled tempered glass on front and back surfaces. Recycled glass provides structural rigidity.

  • Thickness Targets: 0.6–1.0 mm tempered glass panels.
  • Recycled Target: Evaluating 60%+ post-industrial or post-consumer glass cullet.
  • Reinforcement Testing: Studying chemical strengthening via ion-exchange.

Nanocellulose (nanofibrillated cellulose from wood pulp or agricultural waste) is studied as an experimental reinforcement and bonding layer.

  • Fibers under 15 nm form hydrogen-bonded networks being studied for tensile properties.
  • Research evaluates thin coatings (approx 0.1 mm) for toughness and optical clarity preservation.
  • Nanocellulose composite trials belong to early Phase 2+ research. Early test rigs utilize standard tempered glass.
Modular Hardware System

Cioré is researching screw-focused primary assembly with bio-based sealing trials to maximize potential repairability.

Evaluated screw targets: M1.6–M2.0 Torx machine screws in stainless steel. Sealing research includes bio-derived removable gels for water resistance testing without permanent structural gluing.

Planned Swappable Modules
  • Battery module (Nyro pack testing)
  • Camera module (two-lens ribbon)
  • Speaker and audio module
  • USB-C port board and vibration motor assemblies

Modular design concepts aim to extend device longevity and enable component replacement without full device disposal.

Display System and Capacitive Controls

The display research evaluates high-contrast OLED layouts with layered visual depth effects.

  • Phase 1–2 Research: Standard OLED panels paired with under-glass visual treatments.
  • Phase 3+ Research: Studying transparent OLED or MicroLED modules for future feasibility.
Capacitive Controls (No Mechanical Buttons)
  • Volume Strip: Capacitive touch strip concept with haptic feedback.
  • Power Bar: Top touch zone for wake/sleep inputs.
  • Battery Mode Toggle: Capacitive zone to evaluate power profile switches.
  • All touch control concepts pair with haptic and audio signals for accessibility evaluation.
Camera System

Rear Camera Concept: Two-lens vertical arrangement flush with the back profile.

Front Camera Concept: Under-glass or visually integrated lens placement.

Back Panel Concept: Smoked dark panel that is opaque under standard light but reveals internal geometry under strong lighting.

Notification Indicator Concept: Soft LED lighting zone for subtle status presence, fully controllable in user settings.

Additional Phone Features
Messaging
Encrypted Messaging System
Exploring end-to-end encrypted messaging research using open protocols. UI concepts like theme accents and voice clip sharing are designed around encrypted standards. Local classification research explores flagging unrecognized sender patterns.
Navigation
GPS and Offline Navigation
Testing offline mapping using OpenStreetMap datasets. Research includes optional location obfuscation options and emergency mesh beaconing over BLE when cellular is disconnected.
Audio
Sound and Audio Experience
Researching directional mic processing, local audio transcription, and offline voice command handling without external cloud dependencies.
Video
Video Calling Research
Exploring E2EE browser-based WebRTC video links that require no client installation for recipients.
Biometrics
Biometric Unlock Methods
Studying secure facial verification models stored strictly in local hardware enclaves. Biometric data remains isolated on device.
OS
Custom OS Features
Testing system focus modes, interface adaptations based on environmental cues, and optional local peer discovery options.
Control
Update and Access Control
Researching clear line-item software update lists with selective deferral options for non-critical patches.
Community Hardware Feedback

Our waitlist and social media community has been actively sharing what they want from the Cioré phone. These are real requests from real people who are waiting for this product. We take this seriously and it informs our research direction. Below is what we are hearing and where things stand.

Audio Jack
Researching
A significant portion of the waitlist wants a physical audio jack. This is one of the most consistent hardware requests we have received. We are researching how to include a 3.5mm audio jack without compromising the modular design or the form factor. For users who use wired headphones, adapters, or external recording equipment, this matters. It is on our list and being evaluated seriously.
Modular Physical Buttons
Researching
The modular design of the Cioré phone opens the door to customizable physical button placement and function. Community feedback has pointed to programmable side buttons, customizable shortcut keys, and the ability to configure hardware controls without software workarounds. We are researching how modular button systems can work within the existing screw-assembly design framework.
Modular Keyboard Attachment
Researching
Some community members want the option of a physical keyboard. A snap-on or slide-out keyboard module would fit within the modular philosophy of the phone and serve users who prefer typing without relying entirely on a touchscreen. We are researching how this would integrate with the hardware and what the technical requirements would be for a first or later phase implementation.
Phone Stand
Researching
A purpose-built stand that works with the Cioré phone's dimensions and solar charging behavior is a natural companion accessory. Community interest has been clear. This could function as both a display stand and a Nyro-compatible solar positioning tool. We are looking at how this fits into the accessory ecosystem.
Case and Form Factor Variations
Researching
Not everyone connects with the same phone shape. We have received feedback from community members who want different casing options, including different grip profiles, rounded versus flat edge variations, and alternative materials. The modular nature of the phone makes this more feasible than on a traditional sealed device. We are researching what variations make sense for a first release and what could come later as the product line grows.
ⓘ
These are active research areas informed directly by what our community is asking for. If you have background in hardware design, materials research, or product engineering and want to contribute to any of these, reach out on Discord or check the open workstreams on the Working On Now page.

1. Lumé OS: Start Here

Lumé OS is Cioré's operating system research project. It is being planned as an open, hardened operating environment designed to put users in direct control of app permissions, background behaviors, and data choices. Cioré is currently researching requirements, evaluating open-source bases, and designing initial user interaction patterns.

2. Current Direction

The current direction for Lumé OS focuses on establishing a secure, performant proof of concept built on a hardened open-source Android (AOSP) foundation. This approach allows Cioré to test privacy enforcement and user interface concepts on real reference hardware while longer-term architecture options are evaluated.

3. Lumé OS Kickoff

The Lumé OS kickoff phase organizes initial requirements, build environments, reference hardware choices, and contributor tasks. This phase establishes the foundation for early testing, developer workflows, and documentation.

4. Planning Map

The Lumé OS planning map outlines the progression from initial requirements gathering to prototype testing. All stages are subject to engineering, security, hardware, and compatibility review.

Stage 1
Requirements & Reference Setup
Defining security targets, threat models, and setting up build workstations with reference test devices.
Stage 2
Hardened Base Validation
Compiling and testing a hardened AOSP base on reference hardware to verify boot integrity and hardware driver support.
Stage 3
Privacy Layer & Interface Prototype
Developing Cioré's custom permission enforcement rules, sandboxing boundaries, and plain-language privacy dashboard.
Stage 4
Compatibility & User Research
Testing app isolation tiers, evaluating optional compatibility features, and conducting community usability reviews.
5. Why AOSP First

Building on a hardened AOSP foundation for early testing allows Cioré to leverage established hardware driver support, verified boot mechanisms, and security research. This provides a stable environment to build and test Cioré's unique privacy enforcement layer and user interface without needing to construct fundamental hardware drivers from scratch.

6. Workspace Setup

Setting up a Lumé OS research workspace requires a high-performance build machine or cloud compilation instance, unlockable reference hardware (such as recent Pixel devices for early testing), and standard Android platform development tools. Detailed setup guides are being compiled for technical contributors.

7. Privacy, Data Control & Permissions

Lumé OS plans to replace vague system permissions with explicit, time-limited, and plain-language choices. Users will be able to see exactly what data an app requests, choose temporary or one-time access, and review clear activity logs in the privacy dashboard.

8. Accessibility & Waitlist Feedback

Community and waitlist feedback directly shapes Lumé OS features. Research includes high-contrast visual modes, customizable font scaling, tactile touch responses, and simplified navigation flows to ensure technology is accessible to all users.

9. Data Transfer & User Transition Research

Cioré is studying safe, user-controlled methods for transferring contacts, media, and essential data from existing mobile platforms to Lumé OS without exposing data to unvetted cloud services or third-party tracking during transition.

10. Research Status & What Is Not Yet Promised
ⓘ
Research Notice: Lumé OS is in early planning and research. No reference hardware, release timeline, budget, or final feature set is finalized. Specifications and architecture directions are subject to security, engineering, and hardware validation. This is not yet a product promise.

Gaia: AI Transparency & User Control Research

Cioré is studying Gaia as an AI transparency and user-control research direction designed to help people understand AI activity inside apps and make informed choices.

Origin and Evolution of Gaia

Gaia originally began as research into ethical, on-device AI and related infrastructure concepts. As Cioré gathered feedback from waitlist subscribers and community members, a clear consensus emerged: users do not want forced AI, mandatory assistants, or background AI behavior running without their explicit knowledge and consent.

Current Research Focus

In response to community feedback, Cioré reframed Gaia into a research direction centered on transparency and user control. Rather than acting as a background automation layer, Gaia explores how an operating system can give users clear visibility into when apps trigger AI processes, what data those processes request, and how to grant or revoke permission in plain language.

Nyro: Energy & Thermal Research
Nyro is Cioré's research initiative into energy handling, battery longevity, thermal management, and safe charging systems for modular mobile technology.
Nyro Overview and Core Goals
  • Researching ethically sourced battery chemistries, avoiding cobalt and unverified supply chains.
  • Exploring modular, user-replaceable battery packs with straightforward swap access.
  • Solar-assisted energy input research: studying how supplemental photovoltaic surfaces can top up energy under specific light conditions.
  • Thermal management research, load balancing, and safe operating temperature guidelines.
Solar-Assisted Charging vs Solar-Powered

Cioré devices are explored as solar-assisted, not solar-powered. Clarifying this distinction establishes accurate expectations for contributors and community members.

Solar-Assisted Explained: Solar-assisted means Cioré is exploring small supplemental energy inputs from light or environmental sources to top up or support battery performance. It does not replace regular charging. USB-C remains the primary charging method. Claiming a phone is "solar-powered" would incorrectly imply that sunlight alone is sufficient for normal phone operation, which is not the case for modern smartphone power demands.
What Solar Assistance Is Being Researched To Deliver
  • Under favorable outdoor sunlight, supplemental PV surfaces are studied to evaluate small energy gains that may extend standby time.
  • Transparent and back-panel PV materials are evaluated for slow trickle charging under direct sunlight.
  • Indoors and in standard ambient light, supplemental energy gains are minimal and serve primarily as research into low-power harvesting.
  • USB-C remains the primary, reliable daily charging method for all Cioré device concepts.

Battery module concepts focus on user-accessible removal via mechanical fasteners. Research includes studying closed-loop take-back workflows to ensure safe material recovery and recycling at end-of-life.

Regulatory & Legal Boundaries
⚖
Radio transmission is strictly regulated by government agencies (such as the FCC). Cioré's research adheres strictly to legal spectrum limits, ISM unlicensed bands, and public safety guidelines. Transmission on licensed emergency or public safety frequencies is prohibited. Cioré does not promise production transmission range or emergency reliability features.

Research

This page is where you can follow the research Cioré is doing as we build toward the phone and the operating system. Research here is real, sourced, and done in plain language. As the founder works through each topic, notes and findings are added here so the team can stay informed and contribute.

Why We Research Before We Build

Building a privacy-first phone means understanding what you are protecting people from. That requires studying how current phones actually work, what existing privacy systems do well, where the gaps are, and what is technically feasible for a company at our stage. This is not theoretical. Every research area here connects directly to a decision we will eventually make.

Current Research: AI and Privacy in 2026

Research date: May 29, 2026. This research covers what kinds of AI are actually running inside phones right now, how apps embed them, what data they touch, and what real user control looks like technically. The goal was to understand the landscape clearly enough to explain it in plain language and make good product decisions from it.

Main finding: The strongest opportunity is not just saying we value privacy. It is giving people clear control over AI: showing when apps use it, what data it touches, and what they can turn off, limit, or block. No competitor is doing this in plain language, at the OS level, for everyday users.
What Kinds of AI Are Actually Inside Phones
Researched
Two types matter. On-device AI runs entirely on your phone's chip, nothing leaves. Cloud AI sends your data to a company's server, gets a result back, and returns it. Most privacy risk lives in that round trip. Generative AI (chatbots, image filters, voice assistants) is what most people think of, but predictive AI has been inside apps for over a decade, watching what you tap and how long you pause, mostly invisible. Android 16 now runs AI on notifications at the OS level before you even see them, turned on by default.
How Apps Embed AI and What Data They Touch
Researched
App developers do not build AI from scratch. They drop in pre-built toolkits from Google, Meta, OpenAI, and others. Each toolkit has its own data collection rules that you never agreed to. An app can contain four or five of these toolkits simultaneously. The data these systems most commonly need includes voice and audio, photos, behavioral patterns like what you tap and how long you pause, and everything you type. The choice to use cloud AI is a business decision, not a technical requirement. Many apps use it because it is easier and cheaper, not because it is the only option.
What Existing Privacy Operating Systems Do and Where They Stop
Researched
GrapheneOS has the strongest security and the most rigorous sandboxing. CalyxOS balances usability with solid protection. Murena is the most mainstream-friendly and closest to what Cioré is building for. Purism is a cautionary example of overpromising on delivery. The gap all of them share: none of them have a product story, a feature, or plain-language communication specifically about AI inside apps. That conversation has been left entirely on the table. Cioré is entering a market that has not addressed what users are actually asking about right now.
Technical Possibilities for Detecting AI in Apps
Researched
Two methods exist. Static scanning reads the app file before it runs and looks for known AI toolkit signatures. A French nonprofit called Exodus Privacy already does this for tracking toolkits and is actively maintained. Network monitoring watches what an app connects to while running. Known AI services have recognizable web addresses, and tools like TrackerControl can already see these connections, they just do not label them as AI for regular users. The hardest problem is translation. Vague warnings get ignored. Specific, plain-language labels change behavior. Research from Carnegie Mellon confirmed this: specificity works, generic warnings do not.
What Real User Control Actually Means Technically
Researched
Controls range from strong to symbolic. Cutting an app's network access entirely is the strongest: if it cannot reach a server, it cannot send your data there. Blocking specific server addresses is also strong and more surgical. Permission revocation is medium strength because it stops new collection but not use of data already collected. Privacy policy opt-outs are the weakest because they rely on the company's honesty and have no technical enforcement. Cioré's goal is to give users real valves, not the appearance of valves.
The Legal Landscape in 2026
Researched
The EU AI Act is the most important regulation currently in effect. Since February 2025, certain AI practices are banned in the EU including social scoring and manipulation below user awareness. Starting August 2026, any AI system that interacts with users must disclose it is AI. AI-generated content must be labeled. These requirements apply to any company whose product is used in the EU, regardless of where the company is based. Cioré's planned approach of surfacing AI activity to users is aligned with where this regulation is going. Opt-in is also becoming law: several jurisdictions are moving toward requiring AI features to be off by default.
The Competitive Landscape and Where Cioré Fits
Researched
The privacy phone market exists and is real, but it is built for technically sophisticated users. Most people cannot complete the setup process for GrapheneOS. CalyxOS is easier but still requires manual setup. Murena is the closest to a mainstream product. Nobody in this market is specifically positioned around AI. The entire conversation about AI inside apps has been left open. Cioré's opportunity is combining solid technical protection with communication and usability that works for everyday people, not just developers and journalists.
Key Phrases From the Research
On-device AI is like a calculator built into your phone. It thinks for itself. Nothing leaves. Cloud AI is like calling someone at a company, reading them everything you just did, having them figure it out, and telling you the answer. That call is the privacy risk.
Plain Language
Your free app has four companies inside it that you never hired.
This is how we explain third-party AI toolkits embedded in apps without using technical terms.
Plain Language
Apps are allowed to write their own privacy report. We actually check.
Research on 1.4 million apps found that what apps claimed they collected did not match what they actually did. Detection-based labeling is more reliable than self-reporting.
Plain Language
Existing privacy phones were built for security experts. We built ours for everyone else.
The usability gap is the largest available market in this space and it is almost entirely unserved.
Our Stance
We are not against AI. We are against AI you did not ask for.
This is the honest, grounded position. We use tools. Our community uses tools. The issue is consent, visibility, and control.
